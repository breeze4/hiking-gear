# Personal Items — Hygiene, Skin Care, Trip Recording, Wallet

The small stuff that lives in a hipbelt pocket or a dedicated accessory sack. Source: Skurka "Personal items" section; Clelland Tips 16, 33, 34, 54, 59, 60, 61, 116, 117; *Lighten Up!* "The Small Necessities" chapter (hygiene kit).

**Core principle: do the big rocks first.** Clelland's Tip 16 and Skurka both warn: don't micro-optimize personal-items weight before you've trimmed shelter, pack, sleep system, and food. A cut-down toothbrush is not a base-weight strategy.

**Core discipline: decant everything.** Retail packaging is massively oversized for backcountry use. All three sources repackage liquids, pastes, and ointments into 0.2 oz dropper bottles, eye-drop bottles, or balm jars sized to actual daily use. Ask contact-lens-wearing friends for empty dropper bottles; drugstores and online sources also stock them.

**Storage:** oft-used items (sunscreen, lip balm, bug dope, hand sanitizer) in a hipbelt pocket for day access. Occasionally-used items (toothbrush, toothpaste, soap, medications, journal, wallet) in a small stuff sack at the top of the pack.

## Dental

**Principles**

- **Standard toothbrush is fine.** Skurka cuts his in half for packing, Clelland calls the chopped handle "a badge of honor," *Lighten Up!* says ultralighters "seldom bother." All three explicitly reject fingertip toothbrushes — "gross, especially on a backpacking trip" (Skurka).
- **Toothpaste has several valid forms:** sample-tube paste, tooth powder, homemade toothpaste dots, or Dr. Bronner's as a multi-use substitute. Weight differences are marginal; pick for preference.
- **Floss doubles as gear-repair thread.** Carry unscented/unflavored floss and a needle with a large enough eye to use it as emergency thread for clothing and shoes. String floss > flossing picks for multi-use, but picks keep dirty hands out of your mouth on trail.
- **Brush once a day at minimum** and mechanical scrubbing does most of the work — don't skip the brush to save the 0.5 oz of paste.

**Picks**

- *Any standard toothbrush*, handle trimmed only if it doesn't fit the hygiene sack. Colgate extras from a dentist visit are free.
- *Sample-size toothpaste tube* — the easiest answer; ~1 oz for a multi-day trip.
- *Dr. Bronner's Pure-Castile Peppermint Liquid Soap* — Skurka's substitute. A few drops per brushing, and the same bottle washes hands, sunglasses, wounds, or a stubbornly dirty pot. Decant into a 0.2 oz dropper bottle (~10–14 days of capacity).
- *Clelland's toothpaste dots* — fun project; squirt chalky white paste on a plate, let dry a few days, cut into half-inch segments, store in a Ziploc with baking soda. More amusing than weight-saving.
- *Any unflavored dental floss* — doubles as thread; 0.1 oz is enough for a trip.

## Poop kit

**Principles**

- **Digging tool:** a stout tent stake, a trekking pole tip, or a sharp stick. All three books endorse multi-use — skip the dedicated orange trowel. *Lighten Up!*: "an item doesn't need to be ideal for all of its potential uses; it just needs to get the job done."
- **The cathole:** 6–8 inches deep in organic soil, 200 ft from water, camp, and trails. If the ground is too hard to dig deep, go wider or dig a shallow trench. The goal is decomposition in organic topsoil — not burial in sterile mineral substrate where the waste mummifies.
- **After the deposit, stir the contents into the soil with a stick** (not the tent stake you'll use for the shelter). Dramatically accelerates decomposition. Refill the hole and disguise it.
- **Wiping options:**
  - **Snow** is the gold standard — "crème de la crème" (Clelland). Smooth, abrasive, slightly wet, and white so you can monitor progress. Skurka and Clelland independently endorse it.
  - **Natural materials:** smooth flat river rocks (never round), mullein or lamb's ear leaves, smooth weathered sticks ~1" diameter, old-man's-beard moss, folded grass, "butt-scuff" on dewy grass tufts. Pinecones don't work (Douglas fir cones excepted).
  - **Water bidet:** a 1 L bottle plus a few drops of soap. Clelland's standard practice. One dedicated hand for the clean work (right), one for the dirty work (left) — never cross-contaminate.
  - **Toilet paper:** budget 4 squares per day; use only for final polishing after natural materials or a bidet. **Pack out, don't bury** — animals dig it up and it ruins campsites. Triple-bag for transport.
- **Hand washing after is non-negotiable.** Fecal–oral transmission is the dominant cause of backcountry GI illness. Soap or hand sanitizer, every time. In groups, a teammate fetches soap and water from the pack so contaminated hands don't touch anything.

**Picks**

- *Trowel: a stout MSR Groundhog* or trekking pole — no dedicated trowel needed.
- *Smartwater bottle or similar disposable* — 1 L capacity for the bidet bottle. Swap with a drinking bottle only with a clear marking system (or use a dedicated one).
- *Natural materials* — snow first, then the Clelland catalogue.
- *TP pack-out:* doubled Ziploc for used toilet paper; dust with baking soda to control smell.

## Bathroom assistance (pee rag, funnel, night pee jar)

**Principles**

- **Pee bottle** is situational: fully-enclosed shelter, frigid nights, overactive bladder, or long winter nights where getting out is miserable. Only Skurka addresses it directly.
- **Critical rule:** the pee bottle must be physically distinct from every water bottle in the kit. Don't "mark" a water bottle and hope — use a different shape, material, or color. Drinking your own urine at 2 am is a documented failure mode.
- **Women's urination aids** (GoGirl, Shewee) improve aim and privacy for standing urination. Skurka mentions them; Clelland and *Lighten Up!* don't cover them.
- **Pee rag** (e.g., Kula Cloth): a small antimicrobial cloth for drip-drying after urination. Reduces UTI risk and TP use. Tie to the outside of the pack to air-dry between uses; wash at water sources.

**Picks**

- *Kula Cloth* — antimicrobial pee cloth; widely adopted.
- *Plastic water bottle of a visibly different color or shape* — pee bottle.
- *GoGirl or Shewee* — female urination aid for standing use.

## Hand soap or sanitizer

**Principles**

- **Both work; sanitizer is faster, soap is more thorough.** Skurka uses sanitizer for convenience (accepts the smell and skin-drying). Clelland carries both in tiny dropper bottles. *Lighten Up!* defaults to biodegradable soap.
- **Dr. Bronner's** (unscented for bear country) is the universal UL soap — multi-use for hands, dishes, face, bathing, laundry. A 0.2 oz dropper bottle holds 10–14 days of supply.
- **Wash at least once a day** (after bio breaks and before meals). Clelland: "I wash my hands at least once a day (for hygienically handling food)."
- **In groups, hand hygiene is everyone's problem.** A single hiker who skips hand-washing can take down a whole group with norovirus. Don't be that hiker.
- **Use tiny amounts.** A few drops of soap is plenty — lather hard, don't add more.
- **Wash 200 ft from water sources.** Even biodegradable soap doesn't belong in lakes and streams.

**Picks**

- *Dr. Bronner's Pure-Castile Peppermint Liquid Soap* (unscented for bear country) — Skurka's and Clelland's pick. Decant to 0.2 oz dropper.
- *Purell Advanced Hand Sanitizer* — decanted into a 1-oz squirt bottle.
- *Coghlan's soap sheets* — alternative solid-soap form; a few sheets per trip.

**Principles**

- **Sources are thin on this.** Skurka explicitly defers to an external article by Trinity Ludwig on female hygiene best practices — the book itself doesn't cover it. Clelland and *Lighten Up!* don't address menstruation, pee rags, or hair care.
- **The honest answer is: this row requires external sources,** not these three books. The CSV template lists it as Contingent.
- Practical guidance (not from the source books): menstrual cup (e.g., Diva Cup, Saalt) is the UL favorite for multi-day trips — reusable, odor-controlled, no disposal burden. ## Skin care (sunscreen, lip balm, anti-chafing)

**Principles**

- **Clothing first, sunscreen second.** The most efficient sun protection is a long-sleeve shirt, long pants, and a brimmed hat. Sunscreen is for the gaps (face, neck, backs of hands). This principle cuts how much sunscreen you need to carry by an order of magnitude.
- **Sunscreen spec:** broad-spectrum, water-resistant, SPF 15+ (*Lighten Up!*), SPF 30 is Skurka's default. Apply 30 min before exposure. Reapply every 1.5–2 hours during 10 am–4 pm peak.
- **Lip balm with SPF is the consensus.** Skurka carries a dedicated SPF lip balm. Clelland's luxury item is a tube of Burt's Bees. *Lighten Up!* specifies SPF 15+ minimum.
- **Cold and wind:** switch from liquid sunscreen to Dermatone (Skurka), which is a balm and also buffers the face against wind and frostbite.
- **Anti-chafing: prevention beats treatment.** Skurka's rule: wash groin daily in arid environments, after each bio break in humid ones. Bonnie's Balm (or any petroleum/lanolin balm) as a backup for chapped or already-irritated skin. Clelland carries Gold Bond menthol talc.
- **Decant to actual trip size.** 8 mL (0.25 oz) of sunscreen is enough for a weeklong trip if you wear long sleeves and pants (Clelland). Don't carry a retail tube.

**Picks**

- *Sawyer Stay-Put SPF 30 Sunscreen* — Skurka's pick; 1 and 2 oz tubes.
- *Dermatone* — cold/wind face balm; doubles as lip balm.
- *Burt's Bees Lip Balm* — Clelland's luxury pick.
- *Any SPF 15+ lip balm* — Sun Bum, Aquaphor, Nivea.
- *Bonnie's Balm* — Skurka's chafing backup; small jar.
- *Gold Bond Menthol Talc* — Clelland's chafing powder.

## Eye care

**Principles**

- **Sources are thin here.** *Lighten Up!* covers sunglasses (see `worn.md` for the full treatment). Clelland notes moisturizing eye drops as a desert luxury item. Skurka doesn't address contact lens care.
- **For contact lens wearers:** carry a backup pair of glasses for loss/damage, a small saline bottle decanted into a dropper bottle, and a compact contact case. Don't rely on finding water to rinse lenses — carry saline.
- **For prescription glasses wearers:** a hard case is cheap insurance (see `pack.md` eyewear case section).

**Picks**

- *Refresh or Systane lubricating drops* — Clelland's desert pick.
- *Contact lens case + small saline bottle* — personal-preference territory.

## Trip recording (camera, journal)

**Principles**

- **Camera is a "luxury item" in Clelland's framing** (Tip 60) — the trade you can make once you've trimmed ounces elsewhere. A dedicated camera is not essential; a smartphone fits most needs.
- **Smartphone vs. dedicated camera:** smartphones are good enough for occasional shots and have the advantage of already being in the pack for GPS/communication. Dedicated cameras win on longer battery life, optical zoom, physical controls, less screen glare, and better image quality for serious photography.
- **Camera protection:** LOKSAK dry bag in the hipbelt pocket for light rain. Move to a jacket pocket in hard rain; to the pack liner in torrential rain or when not shooting for hours.
- **Spare batteries and memory cards** for week-to-two-week trips. Longer trips: portable charger (Anker PowerCore), solar, support-team mailing, or town resupply.
- **Tripods:** Pedco and Joby models at 2–4 oz justify their weight if photos matter. Boulders and logs are unreliable alternatives.
- **Journaling:** Skurka writes in map margins — "the geographic context adds color." Blank résumé paper in reserve for prolific moments. Ballpoint pen, not gel (smudges) or pencil (fades). Store a second pen warm in a jacket pocket when it's cold enough to freeze ink.
- **Entertainment (music, e-reader)** is explicitly rejected by Clelland (Tip 33): "media fast has huge benefits for the soul" and MP3 players dull bear-country perception. Skurka and *Lighten Up!* don't address it. No positive source-backed guideline exists for entertainment devices.

**Picks**

- *Smartphone* — default camera for most hikers, especially if it's already in the kit for GPS.
- *Sony RX100 series* — Skurka's dedicated camera pick; 2.8× DSLR-class sensor, excellent manual controls. ~9–11 oz.
- *Canon PowerShot S or G series* — Skurka's alternative dedicated camera picks.
- *LOKSAK 6-inch dry bag* — camera protection in the hipbelt pocket.
- *Pedco UltraPod II* or *Joby GorillaPod* — tiny tripods if photos matter.
- *Pilot G2 or any black/blue medium ballpoint pen* — journaling pen.

## Wallet

**Principles**

- **Strip to essentials:** ID, one credit card, some cash. Leave the gift cards, punch cards, and receipts at home.
- **Skurka:** card-holder-style wallet or sandwich bag, low profile, light.
- **Clelland:** leave the wallet in the car entirely (hide under the seat with other valuables; for theft concerns, walk the car-stuff off into the bushes). On a long enough trip, you don't need it in the pack at all.
- **Trailhead car-storage practice:** tuck valuables under a seat, lock the car, hide the key under a bumper or in a tiny rock-pile cache. Don't leave anything visible.

**Picks**

- *Sandwich Ziploc or small stuff sack* — the lightest wallet.
- *Chums Surfshorts Wallet* — template default compact wallet.
- *Thin card-holder or money clip* — Skurka's pick.
